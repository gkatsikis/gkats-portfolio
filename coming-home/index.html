Coming home

νόστος · nostos, the journey home

Four practices that nourish me, and that made me. Reading slowly, eating together, falling well, sitting still. Underneath, they have always been one practice, connection.

The Greeks had a word for the journey home, νόστος. Homer built the Odyssey around it. The Odyssey and One Piece share a shelf here for a reason. Both stories epitomize fighting for your dreams, keeping your friends close, and doing what is right. Odysseus crossed the world to get back to his people. Luffy brings his along.

02

Sharing the table

Mexican food is both my favorite cuisine and my culture. I take real pride in it and its mix of complexity and history. Oaxaca has my whole heart, the moles, the markets, the idea that a dish can carry centuries and still be dinner.

I come from two cultures that agree on one thing above all. Nobody leaves the table hungry. Greek philoxenia and Mexican hospitality are the same instinct wearing different aprons; the guest is sacred, and the food is how you say so. That belief runs deep in me. Food is warmth, food is love, and food is a human right, not a privilege.

Nobody taught this better than Anthony Bourdain. Sit down, eat what you are served, listen. Half of understanding anyone is sharing their table.

03

Training on the mats

Black belt, earned the slow way.

The story starts in 2009, in a Royce Gracie affiliated gym, and the blue belt came from Royce himself. A year in Japan followed, training judo at International Budo University and learning how to fall before anything else. Back home, Marcio Stambowsky at Gracie Sports tied on my purple and brown belts, and Luis Figueroa at Ronin Jiu Jitsu tied on my black.

I still train at Ronin today, my current focus is takedowns and leglocks

04

Sitting with stillness

This orb breathes the way I practice. In, hold, out, hold. You are welcome to borrow it for a minute; the page is not going anywhere.

Meditation has been the anchor of my life. It has helped me weather many storms, and it is where my peace comes from. It shows up as a daily sit, and as a hundred small moments woven through the day, a breath before a hard conversation, a pause before I answer.

Trained as a meditation teacher; still very much a student.

Also in progress, four languages. Greek (B2), Spanish (B2), Japanese (N4), Chinese (HSK 1).

Slowly, calmly, in that order.
